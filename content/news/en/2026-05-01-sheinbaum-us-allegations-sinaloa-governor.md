Mexican President **Claudia Sheinbaum** has publicly questioned US claims that **Sinaloa Governor Ruben Rocha** has ties to the Sinaloa drug cartel, emphasizing that Mexico will only act on solid evidence to protect national sovereignty. The dispute arose on April 30, 2026, following the unsealing of a US indictment in New York charging Rocha and nine others with facilitating cartel operations, including during his 2021 gubernatorial campaign. This development, reported by [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/30/mexico-sheinbaum), underscores ongoing friction between Mexico and the United States over cross-border drug trafficking and political interference.

## Background on the US Indictment

The US prosecutors' case centers on allegations that Rocha and several current and former officials collaborated with leaders of the Sinaloa Cartel to smuggle large quantities of narcotics into the United States. In return, the officials purportedly received political support and bribes. Key accusations involve the cartel's "**Chapitos**" faction—led by the sons of imprisoned co-founder **Joaquin “El Chapo” Guzman**—who allegedly backed Rocha's 2021 campaign through voter interference tactics. These included stealing ballots and ballot boxes, as well as kidnapping and intimidating opposition candidates.

The indictment highlights how these officials used their positions to shield cartel activities, allowing the movement of drugs across the border. This fits into a broader US effort to dismantle the Sinaloa Cartel, one of Mexico's most powerful organized crime groups. Recent high-profile arrests include **Ovidio Guzman**, captured by Mexican forces in 2023 and extradited to the US, **Joaquin Guzman Lopez** now in US custody, and longtime leader **Ismael “El Mayo” Zambada**, apprehended in 2024. These captures have provided US authorities with potential insights into cartel-government links, though specifics remain unconfirmed in public records.

Rocha, a longtime member of the ruling Morena party and former senator, has vehemently denied the charges. In a statement, he described them as "unfounded and politically driven," vowing to challenge them and prove their lack of basis. He framed the accusations as an attack not just on him, but on the "**Fourth Transformation**" movement—a set of national development policies championed by former President **Andres Manuel Lopez Obrador**, under whom Rocha remains aligned.

## Sheinbaum's Stance and Mexico's Legal Position

In her response on April 30, 2026, Sheinbaum reiterated Mexico's commitment to "truth, justice, and the defence of sovereignty." She argued that the US claims lack credibility without backing evidence and suggested they may be politically motivated. "We will not shield anyone who has committed a crime; however, if there is no clear evidence, it is evident that the aim of these charges by the [US] Department of Justice is political," she stated.

Sheinbaum directed Mexico's Attorney General’s Office to review any evidence under Mexican law. If irrefutable proof emerges—either from the US or domestic investigations—the office must proceed accordingly. This approach positions Mexico as unwilling to act on foreign accusations alone, prioritizing its judicial independence. Analysts note that Sheinbaum appears to be buying time, deflecting immediate pressure from Washington while assessing the situation.

**Vanda Felbab-Brown**, an expert on non-state armed groups at the Brookings Institution, observed that the Mexican president's comments effectively demand a thorough review by the attorney general before any decisions. She warned that declining to act without sufficient evidence could heighten bilateral tensions. Extraditing those indicted might yield more details on corruption within Mexico's government and Morena party, potentially drawing from interrogations of captured cartel figures like the Guzmans and Zambada.

## Implications for Mexico-US Relations

This case arrives as Mexico intensifies its own operations against drug cartels, conducting high-profile raids to target powerful groups. However, the US indictment risks straining relations between the two neighbors, who share a 3,000-kilometer border and cooperate on security issues. Past extraditions, such as Ovidio Guzman's, have shown Mexico's willingness to collaborate, but demands for evidence in political cases like this one highlight sensitivities around sovereignty.

The allegations also spotlight Sinaloa state, a notorious hotspot for cartel activity due to its rugged terrain and historical ties to the drug trade. While the source does not detail immediate impacts on daily governance, the scandal could erode public trust in local leadership, especially in a region already grappling with violence.
