# US and Mexico Deny CIA Lethal Role in Anti-Cartel Operation

In a joint statement issued on May 12, 2026, high-ranking officials from the United States and Mexico categorically denied reports suggesting that the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) played a lethal role in a recent operation targeting drug cartels within Mexican territory. The denial, covered extensively by **The New York Times**, addresses growing speculation about the extent of US involvement in Mexico's ongoing battle against organized crime, emphasizing that the collaboration remained strictly within legal and non-lethal parameters. This development comes amid heightened scrutiny of bilateral security efforts, particularly in regions plagued by cartel violence, and underscores the delicate balance of sovereignty and partnership between the two nations.

## Background on US-Mexico Security Cooperation

The context for these denials stems from longstanding US-Mexico efforts to combat drug trafficking and cartel activities, which have been a cornerstone of bilateral relations for decades. Operations like the Mérida Initiative, launched in 2008, have provided Mexico with billions in aid for equipment, training, and intelligence sharing to dismantle criminal networks. However, allegations of overreach by US agencies, including the CIA, have periodically surfaced, often fueled by leaks or investigative journalism.

According to the report in **The New York Times**, the specific operation in question involved Mexican federal forces conducting raids in cartel strongholds, with US support limited to intelligence gathering and logistical advice. Officials from both countries stressed that no CIA personnel were directly involved in any kinetic actions—meaning no participation in arrests, seizures, or lethal force. Mexican Foreign Minister Alicia Bárcena and US Ambassador to Mexico Ken Salazar jointly affirmed this position during a press briefing in Mexico City, clarifying that the partnership adheres to mutual respect for national jurisdictions.

What is known from public records is that such collaborations have intensified in recent years due to the evolving threat of cartels, which control vast swaths of territory and contribute to violence that spills over borders. For instance, widely reported incidents, such as the 2019 capture of Ovidio Guzmán in Sinaloa, involved US intelligence but no direct operational lethal roles. The current denial appears to counter unverified claims circulating in media and online forums, possibly originating from anonymous sources within security circles. Importantly, neither side has released detailed operational logs, leaving some aspects opaque, but the official line remains firm: the CIA's role was advisory only.

This episode highlights the challenges in transparent international cooperation. Mexico has historically been sensitive to perceptions of US interference, a sentiment rooted in past events like the 1990s-era operations that blurred lines between intelligence and enforcement. The **New York Times** article notes that while denials are standard in such cases, they serve to rebuild trust and prevent diplomatic friction.
